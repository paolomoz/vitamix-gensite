## 1. Brand Philosophy

We are here for the Essentialist -- the discerning individual who curates their life with thought and care, seeking depth in every detail and significance in every decision.

> Vitamix is more than a blender. It is a culinary instrument for mindful living -- a tool for those who see the kitchen not as a place of routine, but of ritual. Every moment with Vitamix is a chance to elevate the everyday.

## 2. Logo

### Primary Logo
The Vitamix logo is the foundational element of the brand identity. The current version includes the wordmark and the vortex symbol.

### Vortex
The stand-alone vortex is a supplemental graphic, not a replacement for the primary logo. It should only be used where Vitamix branding is already established. Always include the trademark registration mark.

### Clear Space
A minimum amount of clear space must surround the logo, equal to the diameter of one vortex. This keeps text and other distracting elements away from the logo.

### Logo Color Variants
| Variant | Usage |
|---------|-------|
| **Full color** | Preferred option when applicable |
| **White** | On contrasting, saturated backgrounds (Vitamix Red, Black) |
| **Black** | Only for assets printed in black and white |
| **White and grey** | Alternative option for black and white prints |

### Vortex Color Variants
| Variant | Usage |
|---------|-------|
| **Grey** | Preferred; only on white or light backgrounds |
| **White** | On contrasting, saturated backgrounds (Vitamix Red, Black) |
| **Silver Foil** | Print applications only, when extra emphasis required |
| **Red** | On white backgrounds only; alternative to grey |

### Partnership Logo Rules
- Space logos evenly with a vertical line in the center
- Logos separated by a 1pt rule line spanning the height of the tallest logo

### Common Errors (Do NOT)
- Use on low-contrast backgrounds
- Distort, tilt, or apply effects
- Use incorrect colors
- Modify the font
- Invade clear space
- Add taglines to the logo area
- Place on busy parts of photography

## 4. Typography

### Type Hierarchy

| Role | Typeface | Weight | Notes |
|------|----------|--------|-------|
| **Header** | Sentinel | Book, Medium, Bold | Titles, promo messaging, product headings |
| **Subheader** | Sentinel Book | Book (400) | Short form copy support; usually follows header |
| **Body Copy** | Gotham Book / Gotham Narrow Book | Book (400) | Primary reading text |
| **Eyebrow Text** | Gotham Medium | Medium (500) | ALL CAPS above main heading. White or Vitamix Red on web. |
| **Button Text** | Gotham Narrow Book | Book (400) | CTA buttons |

### Font Families

**Sentinel** (Serif)
- Sentinel Book + Book Italic
- Sentinel Medium + Medium Italic
- Sentinel Bold + Bold Italic

**Gotham** (Sans-Serif)
- Gotham Light
- Gotham Book
- Gotham Medium
- Gotham Bold

### Typography Reminders
- Dollar signs ($) in headlines/subheads: always half the size of the number, aligned to top
- The word "off" in promo messaging: always lowercase
- Superscript all registration and trademark symbols, aligned to top of text
- Registration/trademark symbols follow after series name where applicable
- Slight variances may occur on .com due to template limitations
- Stylistic exceptions may occur based on creative needs

## 5. Photography & Video

### General Principles
- Assets are primarily created in-studio for optimal lighting, composition, and accuracy
- Product should be prominently featured and never look dirty, dusty, or scratched
- Show real, whole-food ingredients
- Don't over-prop scenes; keep styling subtle
- Props/decor should never touch or interfere with the machine
- Area must be well lit

### Studio/Video Rules
- Talent should be center frame if featured
- If featuring a recipe, follow instructions and load container in correct order
- For Getting Started/Demo/Training videos: use two cameras (wide shot + close-up on product)

### Social Content Rules
- Keep video clips fluid, not choppy -- polished even on iPhone
- All non-food props must remain clear of the machine
- Keep free space for text, especially for cover photos/intro frames
- Be cognizant of social media "safety zones" (platform icons, captions)
- Use a tripod unless shooting first-person POV
- No religious symbols/icons in holiday content (widely used decor like Christmas trees is OK)

### Content Types
| Type | Description |
|------|-------------|
| **Getting Started** | Post-purchase guides featuring culinary team |
| **Recipe Technique** | Step-by-step blending program guides with text overlay |
| **Step-by-Step** | Static photo assets for carousels, emails, articles |
| **Internal Communication** | CEO announcements, company updates |
| **Retailer Training** | Videos for retail partner store associates |

## 6. Voice & Tone

### Voice (Who You Are -- Never Changes)

**THIS:**
- Sophisticated
- Informed
- Witty
- Discerning
- Confident
- Considered and to the point

**NOT THIS:**
- Stuffy
- Clinical or overly technical
- Jokey!
- Snobby
- Arrogant
- Verbose

### The Elevated Experience-Seeker
> We don't hide our pride. We stand behind our work, proudly claim our place in a league all our own... and operate with a bit of appealing swagger.

> Yes to smoothies. But extra yes to everything beyond them too. We explore and inspire. We understand how enriching cooking, eating and serving are to everyday life. The process of making and sharing is as meaningful as the finished dish.

### How the Voice Should Sound

**NO** to excessive and !!!
**NO** to overly technical.
**NO** to silly and playful.

Bad examples:
- "Finally, introducing a blender that will chop, blend, puree and make all your neighbors jealous!"
- "I scream, you scream, we all scream for ice cream settings!"

**YES** to clean and quick.
**YES** to informed.
**YES** to witty.

Good examples:
- "It has arrived: the crown jewel of the countertop."
- "Your Vitamix comes with a warranty you'll probably never need."
- "For those whose favorite green juice is a malted milkshake."

### Tone (How You Say It -- Changes by Situation)

| Message Type | Tone | Example |
|-------------|------|---------|
| **Product Launch** | Excited and proud (probably without exclamation marks) | "If we could put a drum roll on this page we 100% would. Introducing the_______." |
| **Recipe Ideas** | Inspiring and engaging, inviting | "After the latest green juice? How about our popular spring pea soup, pistachio ice cream or key lime margarita." |
| **Product Recall** | Apologetic and urgent, clear next steps | "We're sorry. Let's make this right and get your Vitamix taken care of and shipped back ASAP." |
| **Features & Benefits** | Focused and informative, people-first | "Nine next-gen lithium-ion batteries give you the power to blend ice, frozen fruit, and other solid ingredients while you're out and about." |

### Clever vs. Clear Scale

| Channel | Clever | Clear | Description |
|---------|--------|-------|-------------|
| Product Detail Page | 0/5 | 5/5 | Focused and informative. Tech and specs, people-first. |
| In-Store Merchandiser | 1/5 | 4/5 | Primarily clear with a touch of personality |
| Email Newsletter | 2/5 | 3/5 | Balanced for clarity and creative engagement |
| Homepage Banner | 3/5 | 2/5 | Engaging language with creative elements |
| Social Media Post | 4/5 | 1/5 | Highly creative, clever phrasing |
| Print Ad | 5/5 | 1/5 | Entirely clever, curiosity-driven |

## 7. Writing Goals & Principles

### To Educate
Communicate the Vitamix position as a premium brand and category leader while promoting the benefits of whole-food nutrition using clear, simple language.

### To Engage
Form a lasting relationship with the customer and foster a conversation that speaks to them where they are today.

### To Inspire
Show the customer what's possible and how Vitamix can have a positive impact on their life.
